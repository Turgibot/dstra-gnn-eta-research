\abstract{\textbf{Purpose:} Accurate Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA) prediction is essential for pervasive digital services that enhance urban mobility, enabling sustainable development via optimized route planning and supporting people's well-being through reduced travel stress. Existing navigation systems base routing algorithms on analysing current traffic conditions and patterns learned from recent past, limiting their reliability under dynamic traffic conditions. This work addresses how vehicle-centered dynamic graph representation combined with explicit route awareness can deliver accurate ETA predictions.

\textbf{Methods:} We present a dynamic spatio-temporal graph neural network integrating vehicle-level dynamics and explicit route intent within a unified Mixture-of-Experts framework. The key innovation is a vehicle-centered graph representation where vehicles are nodes, enabling direct modeling of vehicle interactions and planned route segments. Temporal dependencies are modeled through sequential graph snapshots. Evaluation uses a large-scale simulated dataset spanning four weeks of urban traffic.

\textbf{Results:} The proposed model reduces Mean Absolute Error (MAE) from 260 seconds to 46 seconds, corresponding to an 82\% improvement. Vehicle-centered dynamic graph representation and explicit route awareness are key contributors: route-aware modeling alone achieves 58.4\,s MAE (77.6\% improvement), while the full temporal route-aware variant reaches 46.2\,s MAE with balanced accuracy across short (24.1\,s), medium (38.3\,s), and long (76.9\,s) trip categories. Baselines achieve only 40--50\% gains.

\textbf{Conclusion:} Vehicle-centered dynamic graph representation and explicit route awareness are crucial for accurate ETA prediction, enabling pervasive digital services that improve urban mobility well-being and support sustainable transportation planning. The substantial improvements highlight the benefit of representing traffic systems as dynamic vehicle-centered graphs with explicit route intent.}
