\abstract{\textbf{Purpose:} Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA) prediction underlies route planning, dispatch, and arrival notifications, and inaccurate estimates erode driver trust in suggested routes. Existing systems estimate ETA mainly from current traffic conditions and recent history, without knowledge of a vehicle's own planned route. This work examines whether a vehicle-centered dynamic graph representation combined with explicit route awareness improves ETA prediction accuracy.

\textbf{Methods:} We present DSTRA-GNN (Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Route-Aware GNN), integrating vehicle-level dynamics and explicit route intent within a unified Mixture-of-Experts framework. Vehicles are graph nodes, enabling direct modeling of vehicle interactions and planned route segments, with a GRU-based temporal aggregator processing sequential graph snapshots. We evaluate on two domains: a large-scale SUMO simulation dataset and Porto-G, a real-world graph representation of the Porto taxi trajectory dataset (ECML-PKDD 2015).

\textbf{Results:} Ablation across six variants, averaged over three seeds under the evaluation protocol, shows that route awareness and temporal context each give consistent, compounding gains on both domains. On duration-matched Porto-G, MAE drops from 93.3\,s (static graph only) to 68.1\,s (27\% reduction), performing competitively with strong trajectory-based baselines such as DeepTTE (68.6\,s) and MetaTTE (69.5\,s). On the simulated traffic dataset, MAE drops from 80.1\,s to 54.8\,s (32\% reduction) under the shared protocol range; on the broader p95-filtered SUMO population, the full model remains the strongest evaluated variant and outperforms applicable classical and graph-native baselines at 72.4\,s MAE.

\textbf{Conclusion:} Vehicle-centered dynamic graph representation and explicit route awareness are key to accurate ETA prediction, with consistent gains across simulated and real-world data. The results position graph-snapshot ETA modeling with explicit route state as a competitive alternative to trajectory-native sequence models under a shared evaluation protocol.}
